# Screening — Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run

Authorization: the user said "gas in semua misi yang mepet, lu bisa cek sendiri" and, for execution-risk flags on our own make.fun kit/repo material, "Kalo kena flag gas langsung aja jgn nunggu gw".

Mission: two lines. Line 1 explains one everyday thing to a 10-year-old in a single vivid sentence. Line 2 says in one sentence why the analogy works, naming what it maps and what it leaves out.

Judge criteria, in order:
1. On-topic: explains one everyday thing to a 10-year-old, line 1 one vivid sentence, line 2 on mapping and what is left out.
2. Line 1 analogy concrete and surprising: things a 10-year-old can see, hold or stand in, joined in a way not met before.
3. Line 1 is ONE vivid sentence, picture whole, no list of comparisons, no second metaphor crowding it.
4. Line 2 honest and specific: which parts the analogy covers and which it leaves out, no praising the analogy.
5. The two lines work as a pair, line 2 explaining line 1, language a 10-year-old follows without being talked down to.
Plus the house rule: a vivid picture that leaves a 10-year-old with the wrong idea is worse than a plain one that holds.

## Candidates

### 1. Rain — sponge
L1: Rain is the sky wringing out a giant wet sponge over your head.
L2: It maps water squeezed out of clouds and falling down, but it leaves out that nobody is wringing anything, clouds are not sponges, and rain can fall for hours with no refill.
Score: C1 pass. C2 strong (sponge you can hold) but a familiar image, not fresh. C3 pass, one metaphor. C4 strong and honest. C5 pass. Weakness: the sponge join is well worn, and the picture risks the wrong idea that clouds hold water like a sponge.

### 2. Toaster — sunbed
L1: A toaster is a little sunbed where your bread lies down to get brown.
L2: It maps heat turning pale bread brown, but it leaves out that there is no sun, the heat comes from glowing wires, and your bread pops up when it is done.
Score: C1 pass. C2 middling: a 10-year-old may never have seen a sunbed, so the concrete anchor is weak. C3 pass. C4 good. C5 pass. Weakness: "sunbed" is not something most kids can see or hold.

### 3. Traffic jam — queue for ice cream
L1: A traffic jam is a line of cars standing bumper to bumper like shoppers queuing for ice cream.
L2: It maps everyone waiting in turn with nowhere to go, but it leaves out that cars cannot step out of line, and nobody at the front is handing anything out.
Score: C1 pass. C2 weak: explaining a jam with a queue is near-circular, and the ice-cream queue is a second comparison crowding the picture. C3 fails on the extra comparison. C4 decent. Weakness: two comparisons in line 1, low surprise.

### 4. Washing machine — merry-go-round
L1: A washing machine is a merry-go-round for dirty socks, spinning them round in soapy water until the mud lets go.
L2: It maps the spinning that shakes dirt loose, but it leaves out the soap and hot water doing the real cleaning, and that your socks come out wet, not dizzy.
Score: C1 pass. C2 strong (merry-go-round a child can ride, socks a child can hold). C3 pass, one metaphor. C4 strong. C5 pass. Weakness: a merry-go-round turns flat and level while a washer tumbles, so the picture is slightly off, and line 2 has to spend words fixing it.

### 5. Queue — human train
L1: A queue is a human train, everyone linked up bumper to bumper and shuffling forward one carriage at a time.
L2: It maps the order and the one-by-one forward shuffle, but it leaves out that there is no engine pulling you, no track, and anyone can step out of line.
Score: C1 pass. C2 good (train a child can ride). C3 pass. C4 good. C5 pass. Weakness: "human train" is a known classroom image, surprise is middling, and a queue is already one of the brief's own examples.

### 6. Zipper — teeth
L1: A zipper is a row of tiny metal teeth that bite shut to keep the cold out.
L2: It maps the teeth locking together to close a gap, but it leaves out that the teeth never chew, the little slider does all the work, and the teeth cannot bite you.
Score: C1 pass. C2 good (teeth you can touch). C3 pass, one metaphor. C4 good. C5 pass. Weakness: "teeth that bite" is a very familiar image, surprise is low, and "bite shut" slightly misleads about how a zipper works.

### 7. Umbrella — roof on a stick
L1: An umbrella is a little roof you carry on a stick so the rain slides off your head instead of soaking you.
L2: It maps the roof keeping rain off, but it leaves out that a real roof never folds up, never blows inside out, and never fits in your bag.
Score: C1 pass. C2 middling: "roof you carry" is plain, not surprising. C3 pass. C4 good. C5 pass. Weakness: low surprise, reads like a definition with a roof in it.

### 8. Fridge — cold cave
L1: A fridge is a cold white cave where your milk hides from the warm air so it stays fresh.
L2: It maps the cold keeping food fresh, but it leaves out that the cave has a light, hums all day, and your milk is not hiding from anything.
Score: C1 pass. C2 good (cave a child can stand in). C3 pass. C4 good, and the "not hiding" correction is honest. C5 pass. Weakness: "hides" plants a wrong idea (food hiding) that line 2 must undo, and "cave" for a fridge is a familiar image.

### 9. Microwave — jumping beans
L1: A microwave is a little box full of invisible jumping beans that shake your soup warm.
L2: It maps the shaking that heats food from the inside, but it leaves out that there are no beans, there is no fire, and metal must never go in.
Score: C1 pass. C2 strong and surprising (beans you can hold). C3 pass, one metaphor. C4 strong. C5 pass. Weakness: "jumping beans shake your soup warm" is a fun picture but the mechanism is wrong enough that a child could walk away thinking something rattles inside the box.

### 10. Traffic light — robot eyes
L1: A traffic light is a robot with three coloured eyes that blinks red to stop you and green to let you go.
L2: It maps the coloured signals telling you when to stop and go, but it leaves out that the robot has no brain, it only follows a timer, and it cannot see you at all.
Score: C1 pass. C2 middling: mostly a literal description with "robot" pasted on, low surprise. C3 pass. C4 good. C5 pass. Weakness: the analogy is thin, almost no join, and a child could conclude the light watches them.

### 11. Elevator — bucket in a well
L1: An elevator is a small room that climbs up and down inside a building like a bucket in a well.
L2: It maps the room moving between floors on a rope, but it leaves out that there is no water, no one pulls the rope by hand, and the room stops exactly where you press.
Score: C1 pass. C2 middling: many 10-year-olds have never seen a well bucket, so the anchor is weak. C3 fails slightly: "room that climbs" plus "bucket in a well" is a second comparison crowding the first. C4 decent. Weakness: weak anchor, crowded line 1.

### 12. Escalator — staircase that walks (WINNER)
L1: An escalator is a staircase that walks for you, carrying your shoes upstairs while you stand still and hold the rail.
L2: It maps the steps moving so you do not have to climb, but it leaves out the motor and chain underneath, and that it only goes one way at one steady speed.
Score: C1 pass. C2 strongest: a staircase is something a child stands on every day, "walks for you" joins it to the child in a way not met before, and every noun (stairs, shoes, rail) is touchable. C3 pass: one sentence, one metaphor, picture whole. C4 strongest: names the mapped part (steps moving, no climbing) and two concrete left-outs (motor and chain, one way at one speed), no praise. C5 pass: line 2 directly corrects line 1's one untrue verb ("walks") with the motor, so the pair teaches instead of repeating. Wrong-idea risk is lowest: nothing in line 1 is false except the walking verb, and line 2 owns it.

## Winner

Candidate 12, the escalator. It wins on criteria order: it is on-topic (C1), it has the most surprising concrete join of a thing a child can stand on (C2: a staircase that walks), a single clean metaphor with no crowding (C3), the most specific honest mapping line naming both what maps and what is left out (C4), and the pair works because line 2 corrects line 1's only untrue word (C5). It also beats the merry-go-round washer and the rain sponge on the house rule: the escalator picture cannot leave a child with a wrong idea once line 2 names the motor, while the sponge and the merry-go-round both need line 2 to repair a misleading picture.

## Final entry

Line 1: An escalator is a staircase that walks for you, carrying your shoes upstairs while you stand still and hold the rail.
Line 2: It maps the steps moving so you don't have to climb, but it leaves out the motor and chain underneath, and that it only goes one way at one steady speed.
